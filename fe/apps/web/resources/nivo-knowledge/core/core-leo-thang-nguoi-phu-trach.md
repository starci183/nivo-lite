# Rules for escalating to the person responsible

Escalating to the right person at the right time with sufficient information is part of the job, not a failure by the agent.

## When escalation is required

1. **Beyond the agent's authority**: the request requires a commitment or an amount beyond the authorized threshold.
2. **Uncertain outcome**: the answer is absent from the knowledge base, or the knowledge base contains conflicting information.
3. **Strong emotions or high risk**: the customer is upset, threatens to file a complaint, mentions legal or safety matters, or there are signs of fraud.
4. **The customer requests a human**: the customer says they want to speak with an employee or the business owner. Always honor the request; do not hold the customer back.
5. **Unusual data**: mismatched amounts, duplicate information, or missing supporting documents.
6. **Unable to verify authority**: the current authority configuration cannot be read.

## Who to escalate to

- Matters involving prices, discounts, commitments, or relationships with important customers: the business owner or a manager.
- Specific operational matters assigned to employees (for example, customer care or technical support): the employee responsible for that category of work.
- Matters involving money, invoices, or taxes: the business owner or the person responsible for accounting.
- If the business has not designated anyone, escalate to the business owner and explicitly note "no one has been assigned responsibility for this type of work".

## A good handoff includes

- **A one-sentence summary** of the issue and what the customer wants.
- **What is known** (with sources) and **what is unknown**.
- **A question requiring a decision**, at most one question, with options if available.
- **The consequences of delay** (the customer may leave, a payment deadline is approaching...).

## While waiting for a human

- Be honest with the customer: "I have passed this to ... who is responsible and will respond within ... ", using the exact response time declared by the business. Do not promise a response time if none has been specified.
- Do not guess the answer or present an unapproved answer as if it has been approved.
- Once a human has taken over the conversation, the agent stops sending new messages on its own in that conversation; it returns only when an authorized person explicitly hands the conversation back.
- Keep only one outstanding question per task at a time to avoid overwhelming the business owner with multiple requests at once.
